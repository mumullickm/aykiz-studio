How to count your cat's resting breathing rate

Reviewed 2026-07-16 · Not a substitute for veterinary advice

A useful home check

Short answer

Count how many times your cat's chest rises and falls in 30 seconds while it is asleep or fully resting, then double it for breaths per minute. Under 30 is normal. Consistently above 30 to 40 at rest, or any laboured or open-mouth breathing, means call a vet. It is one of the simplest and most useful home checks there is.

Why the resting breathing rate matters

The number of breaths a cat takes per minute while resting is one of the few heart and lung signals an owner can measure at home, and vets often ask owners to track it for cats with heart or airway concerns. A resting rate that climbs over time can be an early sign of fluid around the lungs, before a cat looks distressed.

How to count it correctly

  1. Wait until your cat is asleep or fully settled and not purring. Purring, activity, heat, and stress all push the rate up and make the count meaningless.
  2. Watch the chest. One breath is one full rise and fall together.
  3. Count the breaths for 30 seconds, then multiply by two for the per-minute rate.
  4. Do it a few times on different days to learn your cat's normal resting number.

What is normal, and what is not

  • Under 30 breaths per minute at rest is normal for most cats, often in the low twenties.
  • Consistently 30 to 40 at rest is worth a vet conversation, especially if it is rising.
  • Over 40 at rest, or any laboured, open-mouth, or belly-heaving breathing, needs a vet now.

One slightly high reading on a warm or stressful day is usually nothing. A rate that is repeatedly high at true rest is the signal that matters.

When fast breathing is an emergency

Open-mouth breathing or panting in a cat that is not hot or just-exercised, gasping, blue or grey gums, or breathing with obvious belly effort are emergencies. Do not delay, and keep the cat as calm and cool as possible on the way to care.

Common questions

Should I count breaths when my cat is awake?

No. Count only when your cat is asleep or fully resting and not purring. An awake, active, warm, or purring cat breathes faster, so the number would not reflect the true resting rate.

My cat breathes fast while sleeping. Is that normal?

Brief fast or irregular breathing during dreaming sleep can be normal. What matters is the steady resting rate. If it is repeatedly above 30 to 40 at rest, or looks laboured, see a vet.

How often should I check it?

For a healthy cat, occasionally, to know its normal. For a cat with a known heart or breathing condition, follow your vet's advice, which is often once or twice a day.
